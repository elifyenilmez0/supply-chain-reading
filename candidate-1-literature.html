Candidate 1: shared upstream risk

Literature overlap and a narrower OM question

Literature check and discussion draft — 20 October 2026. Prepared with reading assistance; to review with Yannis. This is a targeted search and comparison, not a systematic review or a claim that a gap has been proved.

My revised assessment

The starting question is important, but the basic mechanism is already established. Different direct suppliers can share an upstream source; this can change optimal mitigation and transmit financial risk. Neither this observation, adding inventory to it, nor asking whether information has value is sufficient novelty.

My more defensible question to investigate is:

When does verifying a hidden common upstream dependency create usable diversification, once a buyer must qualify and activate an alternative before its inventory buffer is exhausted?

“Usable” is a working description, not a new construct that I can claim to have invented. I mean a product-specific alternative with verified relevant upstream exposure, acceptable quality, available capacity and a feasible activation date. A firm name, different country or predicted link alone does not establish that.

The potential contribution would be a new, well-measured operational fact and a decision mechanism, supported by a convincing comparison with existing models. Its novelty is still conditional. Full-text comparison with the closest information papers is unfinished because several manuscripts could not be accessed.

The 2015 working paper Risky Suppliers or Risky Supply Chains? is an earlier version of the Wang line of work, not a second independent discovery. I use the published 2021 article and its four-author metadata. Likewise, Resilience in Vertical Supply Chains is the earlier title of the QJE paper already in my library; I reused that record.

The closest obstacles to novelty

Ang et al. is the first benchmark. Its model already makes a common tier-2 source affect the buyer’s mitigation strategy and suppliers’ incentives. The precise baseline information assumption appears on printed p. 2401 (PDF p. 6). I cannot describe the basic diamond structure or a stock/sourcing comparison as my contribution.

The information question is also crowded. Bakshi–Mohan and its 2025 companion directly challenge the idea that “we add an audit” is enough. The latter’s abstract even reports a non-obvious comparative static for perfect-information value. I must obtain both full texts before making a theorem-level novelty claim.

Measurement and action are established concerns too. Culot warns about interpreting corporate relationships as relevant product flows, Mungo discusses substantive evaluation, and the September 2026 JBL paper addresses conversion of visibility into action. The remaining opportunity needs a specific decision, observable constraint and result beyond those general messages.

The candidate contribution I would test first

A working title is “From supplier diversification to a ready alternative: the operating value of verifying shared upstream dependence.” This is a project description, not a submission-ready title.

I would narrow to one input family and one critical process stage. The initial decision maker is one buyer. Before a disruption, it can spend a limited budget on verifying a suspected common dependency, qualifying an independently exposed alternative, or holding a buffer. Qualification takes time; a claimed alternate supplier may lack the required grade, process approval or reserved capacity.

The proposed mechanism is that information and readiness can be complements. Learning the correct bottleneck may be valuable only when it changes a feasible action in time. Conversely, qualification can be wasted if the nominal alternative relies on the same constrained source. These are hypotheses to test against the literature, not results established by this review.

A small example helps state the distinction. If inventory covers 20 days but a genuinely independent alternative needs 60 days for approval, discovering that alternative during the disruption does not protect the first uncovered weeks. An already qualified source may help, but only if it has accessible capacity and does not share the affected bottleneck. These numbers are hypothetical. This example is not a novel theorem; recovery/coverage logic has substantial prior work.

What would make the project more than an incremental model? At least one of the following, with evidence:

  1. A documented decision reversal: the same budget produces a different preferred action once product/site dependence and qualification feasibility replace a supplier-name count. Show when an established, correctly implemented baseline would not already recommend that action.
  2. A credible estimate of the benefit of information conditional on operational readiness, using observed procurement and service outcomes rather than risk perceptions alone.
  3. A quantitatively important, validated mismeasurement result: nominal diversification systematically misstates relevant independence in the selected setting, and correcting it materially changes a real decision. This needs more than showing that some database links are wrong.

I would begin with the second route if we have a partner and decision logs. Otherwise, I would start with a small model and an openly reproducible sector pilot, while being explicit that the pilot does not establish firm-level performance or publication-level novelty.

Evidence and data I would need

The useful unit is buyer–part or material grade–production site–date, not just buyer–supplier firm. Minimum fields are:

  • Direct source and the specific common upstream process/site, with evidence and verification date.
  • Whether an alternative is technically qualified for the same requirement; qualification start/end dates and any approval restrictions.
  • Available or reserved capacity, lead time, inventory coverage and the actual contingency chosen.
  • Disruption timing and operating outcomes: unfilled units, delivery delay, downtime, expediting cost and recovery.

Keep four distinctions separate: unobserved is not independent; common ownership is not necessarily a common physical bottleneck; a shared node is not necessarily relevant to the focal product; and a technically feasible alternative is not necessarily available under widespread stress.

A sample of 10–30 verified part/site cases could expose measurement and access problems before a larger project. That would be a feasibility exercise, not a sufficient sample for a general causal conclusion. A suitable power calculation and dependence structure would be needed for an evaluation design.

A feasible public-data starting point

Cheng et al. provide public code and data and an archived release. The repository has processed flows, raw trade files and analysis scripts. It offers a concrete way to reproduce stage-level dependence and uncertainty bounds. The material is historical and aggregated by country, and some inputs are embedded in scripts. I have verified availability, not run a replication.

The June 2025 correction changes two labels in Fig. 4e and adds a rounding note; it is attached with the corrected paper. A pilot must use the corrected version. Public flows cannot substitute for product qualification or actual reserved-capacity data.

Panjiva or FactSet could provide signals and a sampling frame if our institution has access. Neither should be treated as a complete bill of materials. A missing edge is not proof that two alternatives are independent. The Brazilian transactions used by Monteiro et al. are proprietary; their existence is not evidence that our group can obtain them.

How to distinguish an OM contribution from an algorithm project

The central outcome is an operating decision and its consequences: which verification, buffer or qualification investment should a buyer make, and what service or cost improves? A new optimizer, larger graph or more accurate link classifier would be a tool, not the core claim.

A first model could hold demand and other buyers’ behavior simple and compare:

  • The buyer’s current rule and a nominal supplier-count rule.
  • Known correlated risk with a correctly implemented Ang-style incentive/sourcing benchmark.
  • A Ford-style exposure/contingency baseline once its full model is reviewed.
  • Fixed versus targeted verification; qualification-only and buffer-only policies.
  • A full-information benchmark with the same physical capacity and timing constraints.

Do not handicap the baselines by pretending that the literature assumes independent suppliers when it does not. Report total cost, service, severe shortfall and realized recovery, including verification and qualification costs. Evaluate map errors that disproportionately hide critical links, not only random deletion.

If budget accounting treats audit spending and inventory spending as substitutes, that is a modeling choice to justify, not an empirical fact. The first result should remain visible in a small example; complexity can come later.

A credible empirical route, if data exist

A partner’s phased introduction of verified upstream information could provide a setting, but voluntary adoption is endogenous: better firms may map more, and disrupted firms may map after a problem. Information-arrival timestamps are also often influenced by anticipated risk.

A field design could compare normal planning, verified dependency information, and information plus a defined prequalification support process. Randomization would need to respect shared suppliers and spillovers; withholding already-known safety-critical information would not be an appropriate design. The implementation and sample requirements would have to be agreed with a partner. There is no experiment in place.

Without randomization, a phased rollout or other external variation might support identification only after examining assignment, anticipation, pretrends, demand shifts and concurrent changes. A simple event study does not solve those problems. A descriptive paper should label its estimates as descriptive.

The high-value outcome would connect what was learned → what action became feasible → what was actually changed → what operating consequence followed. Interview explanations can guide the mechanism but cannot replace performance evidence.

Why the broader journals matter

I would borrow the question selection and breadth of these outlets, not assume that a small sourcing model fits all of them.

  • Science: Pichler et al. (2023), already in the library, motivates network visibility as a broad data and coordination problem. Our narrower contribution must identify the buyer action enabled by better information.
  • Nature Communications: Cheng et al. gives a concrete, societally important battery setting. It is not the flagship Nature. A strong sector study would need physical/process knowledge and defensible data, not only a general supplier graph.
  • Nature: Guan’s October 2026 piece is a Comment. It motivates common infrastructure dependencies but is not causal evidence. Digital/energy dependencies are a later application unless we can observe and act on one of them clearly.
  • Econometrica: Kopytov et al. (2024), already in the library, reminds me that safer sourcing and efficient resource allocation are different questions. Lower volatility is not automatically welfare improvement.
  • QJE: Grossman–Helpman–Sabal distinguishes private protective investment from network-wide incentives. A subsidy or public information-sharing proposal would require an explicit externality, not just a buyer saving money.
  • The Economist: Useful for finding contemporary cases and explaining why someone should care. It does not establish a literature gap or identify a causal effect. I did not add a tangential or inaccessible article merely to include the outlet.

A buyer-level operating result could naturally be assessed in OM outlets such as Management Science, MSOM, POM or JOM, depending on method and evidence. A claim about society-wide resilience would need broader validation. No venue or acceptance probability is implied by this review.

What I would say to Yannis

“The closer search changed my view. The common tier-2 problem is already modeled by Ang and coauthors and studied empirically by Wang and coauthors. There are also direct papers on network information and its value. So I would not pitch hidden dependence itself as new. I would like to investigate when learning a product-specific dependency changes a feasible mitigation action, especially when qualifying an independent alternative takes longer than the inventory buffer. I think the first task is to establish whether we can observe that decision and whether it remains distinct from those benchmarks.”

Questions he may ask

Question My answer at this stage
“What exactly is new?” Not yet established. The candidate margin is verified information interacting with readiness for a specific product and bottleneck, with operating outcomes. I must compare the closest full texts first.
“Isn’t this Ang plus an information cost?” It would be too incremental if that is all it becomes. I need a mechanism or empirical decision effect not already implied by its known-configuration model and existing information-value work.
“Isn’t this just time-to-recover versus inventory coverage?” Timing itself is established. The proposed test is how product-specific learning changes which independent response can be qualified in time, evaluated against exposure/contingency baselines.
“Why not map everything?” Full mapping is costly and may not change feasible actions; that general insight already has prior work. We would need to show a specific allocation of verification and readiness effort that changes an important decision.
“Where is the data?” Open battery flows permit a limited pilot. The actual buyer question requires product/site, qualification, capacity and outcome data; we have not confirmed access.
“What if independence cannot be observed?” Then we cannot certify it from missing commercial links. We should narrow to a validated subnetwork or change the question.
“Is this general interest?” The broad problem is whether costly de-risking buys continuity. The contribution would need a compelling fact and a clear operating mechanism. Journal labels cannot supply either.
“Why not model all firms and policy?” That would add a second project. First establish the buyer decision; only then ask whether its incentives differ from network welfare.

First milestones and reasons to stop

  1. Read Ang’s model alongside the full Wang article, Bakshi–Mohan, Bakshi’s companion, the latest Osadchiy manuscript and the Ford implementation. The inaccessible texts are a genuine remaining comparison task.
  2. Write a one-page assumption comparison: known versus uncertain topology; static versus changing links; perfect versus noisy signals; stock position; qualification; actual capacity; decentralized incentives; operating versus financial outcomes. Do not infer absent assumptions from abstracts.
  3. Check whether a firm partner or an existing institutional dataset can supply the minimum fields. Select one input family only after this check.
  4. Reproduce a public-data baseline and a small decision example. Predefine what would count as a meaningful action change.
  5. Stop or pivot if a closest paper already solves the same choice, independence cannot be validated, the decision improvement disappears against a fair baseline, or feasible data cannot measure the operating outcome.
