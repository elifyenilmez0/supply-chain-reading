The question I want to understand

What would it take to observe global firm-level supply networks?

My takeaway

VAT, payment and customs records can reveal complementary portions of a network. Connecting national maps is an institutional and measurement problem as well as a technical one.

Where OM enters

The operational question is which missing information is worth collecting before acting.

What AI changes — and what is not established here

Reconstruction and text extraction can propose links; transaction validation, quantities, product fit and available capacity still matter.

Connection to the other readings

Lee–So–Tang studies the value of a specific demand signal; this article concerns network observability. SPO provides one way to think about decision value.

A question Yannis may ask

Can we simply download these data?

How I would answer at this stage

Usually not. Administrative and bank microdata require access arrangements. Commercial data may be selective. A firm-level edge also does not reveal the actual part or spare capacity.

What I should not overclaim

Policy Forum / mapping proposal, not a newly estimated intervention or proof that complete visibility is optimal.

My short version

The main obstacle is not only optimizing a supply network; it is knowing enough about the network to make a defensible decision. The authors argue for connecting fragmented national firm-level maps into an international map. VAT records, payments and customs data can reveal different parts of the network, but no one source solves the whole problem. Firms often see their own suppliers without seeing several tiers upstream.

How the paper gets there

This is a policy/research proposal, not a new estimated intervention effect. It reviews recent microdata developments and sketches a mapping blueprint. Administrative VAT data can give broad domestic coverage; trade data connect across borders. Commercial links often favor large public firms and have limited transparency. Reconstructing missing links is a useful possibility, but a predicted link is not an observed transaction (PDF pp. 2–5).

Why I am keeping it for an OM project

I would turn the mapping problem into a value-of-information problem. With a small audit budget, which unknown tier-2 relationships should a buyer verify first before choosing suppliers or reserve capacity? The goal would be better decisions with partial information, rather than building a perfect global map. This could pair well with SPO, but the uncertainty in link existence must remain explicit.

What I still need to be careful about

Getting access to VAT or bank records is a major institutional hurdle. A supply link does not reveal quantities, substitutability, inventories or unused capacity. Also, missing links are unlikely to be random. A method that works only when links are hidden uniformly at random might look good in a benchmark and fail on smaller or overseas suppliers.

Questions for Yannis

Can we study targeted supplier verification using a modest, auditable network dataset? What evidence would show that the selected information improves resilience decisions rather than just improving a reconstruction score?
